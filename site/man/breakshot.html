The manual page that comes home with breakshot v0.10.0.

$ tty1 man breakshot

NAME

breakshot — eight-ball pool in the terminal

SYNOPSIS

breakshot [-mute] [-relay url] [-version]

DESCRIPTION

breakshot is eight-ball pool, drawn in the terminal. Play a match for two at one keyboard, play the computer, practice alone, or play someone on another machine online. Everything on screen and every sound is made by code.

The table is seen from above. V flies the camera down behind the cue ball, to aim and shoot from there, and back up again.

The game needs a terminal that shows true color, at least 80 columns by 24 rows. A larger window, or a smaller font, gives a sharper table. In a smaller one the game waits for it to grow, and Esc quits.

The menu offers these, chosen with Up, Down and Enter, or by pointing at one and clicking:

Practice alone
One player. Any ball may be hit, only a potted cue ball is a foul, and the 8 must go last.
Two players
A match for two, taking turns at one keyboard.
Play the computer
You are player 1 and break; the computer plays player 2. It plays straight pots, not banks or safeties.
Host online match
Make a room for a player on another machine; see ONLINE PLAY.
Join online match
Go into a room by its code.
Rules: simple
Switch the next match between the simple rules and the strict ones; see RULES. Online, the host's choice holds for both.
Quit
Leave the game.

Under the noises of the table are the far games of the hall and the murmur of the room.

The options are as follows:

-mute
Play without sound. A machine with no audio device plays without it too.
-relay url
Play online matches through the relay at url. The default is https://relay.usr.games. An empty url, as in -relay "", turns online play off, and the menu leaves out its two online items.
-version
Print the version and quit.

RULES

Eight-ball with simple fouls, and no shot to call.

  • The breaker puts the cue ball down anywhere behind the head string, the line a quarter of the way down the table.
  • The table is open after the break. The first ball potted after it gives the shooter that ball's group, solids (1 to 7) or stripes (9 to 15).
  • Potting one of your own keeps the turn.
  • A foul gives the other player the cue ball in hand, to put down anywhere: potting the cue ball, hitting no ball, or hitting first a ball that is not yours.
  • Pot the 8 after all of your group to win. Potting it earlier, or with a foul in the same shot, loses. On the break it goes back on the table.

The strict rules add to a match:

  • A break that pots nothing must send at least four object balls to a cushion, or it is a foul.
  • After the break, a shot that pots nothing must send some ball, the cue ball too, to a cushion after the cue ball's first hit, or it is a foul.
  • The pocket of the 8 is called, and the 8 in any other loses. The call follows the aim, to the pocket the 8 is sent toward, until P or a click on a pocket calls another; see KEYS.

KEYS

Left, Right
Aim; held, the cue turns faster.
Up, Down
Aim in fine steps.
W, S, A, D
Move the tip, where the cue meets the cue ball: up and down, left and right. Above the middle the cue ball follows on after a hit, below it draws back, and to one side it spins off a cushion that way. The small cue ball on the right rail shows the tip as a red dot. Each shot starts from the middle.
Space
Start the power meter, and press it again to shoot. Where the terminal says when a key is let go, as one with the kitty keyboard protocol does, Space may be held instead: the meter rises while it is down, and letting go shoots. A tap still starts the meter running.
Esc
Call the shot off while the meter runs. Otherwise go back to the menu, and from the menu quit.
V
Fly the camera down behind the cue ball, and back up.
M
Turn the sound off or on.
P
On the 8 under the strict rules, call the next pocket for it. The call then stays until the shot.
Enter
Start another rack once a game is over.

With the cue ball in hand:

arrow keys
Move it.
Enter, Space
Put it down.
C
Pick it up again, before the shot.

The mouse plays too. In the menu, the pointer selects an item and a click chooses it. At the table, from above, move the pointer to aim. Press the left button, drag away from it to set the power, and let go to shoot. With the cue ball in hand, the pointer moves it, a click puts it down, and a click on it picks it up again. On the 8 under the strict rules, a click on a pocket calls it. A click on the small cue ball on the right rail puts the tip there. From behind the cue ball only the keys, and that click, play at the table. Once a game is over, a click on the box that says how it ended does what Enter does.

ONLINE PLAY

Two players on two machines, anywhere, with no ports to open at home:

  1. One chooses Host online match. The game makes a room and shows its code, four letters.
  2. The other chooses Join online match, types the code, and presses Enter. Backspace takes back a letter.
  3. The host breaks. On the other player's turn your keys and the mouse do nothing, and you watch them play.
  4. Esc leaves the room, or the match. At the end, both press Enter for another rack.

The games meet through a small relay on the internet, which passes the shots between them and keeps nothing once a match is over. -relay chooses another one.

ENVIRONMENT

breakshot reads no environment variables of its own. The glonk engine, which draws it, reads these:

COLORTERM
‘truecolor’ or ‘24bit’ says the terminal shows true color, and colors are sent exactly. Without it, or a TERM that says so, each color is sent as the nearest one of the 256-color palette. Inside tmux or over ssh it may not come through; set it where the terminal does show true color.
TERM
A name with ‘truecolor’ or ‘24bit’ in it, or ending in ‘-direct’, says the same.
TERM_PROGRAM
‘Apple_Terminal’ is the Terminal of macOS, which leaves a thin line along the edge of half-block characters. There the picture is drawn in whole cells, a little blockier.

EXIT STATUS

The breakshot utility exits 0 on success, and >0 if an error occurs.
