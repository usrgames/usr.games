Bring a good terminal.

The games are drawn in the cells of your terminal, so the terminal is both the screen and the joystick. Most will play them. A good one plays them better.

Keys you can hold

To walk and turn at once, a game has to know when you let go of a key. Terminals that speak the kitty keyboard protocol tell it:

  • Ghostty
  • kitty
  • WezTerm
  • foot
  • iTerm2
  • Alacritty
  • Rio
  • Warp

In the others only one key stays held at a time. Everything still plays. You just cannot walk and turn together.

All the colours

The games paint in 24-bit colour where the terminal says it has it, and in the nearest of 256 colours where it does not.

Inside tmux or over ssh, the terminal's word often does not arrive. If the colours look banded there, this brings them back:

$ export COLORTERM=truecolor

A big window

Every cell is two pixels, one above the other, so a bigger window or a smaller font is a sharper picture. 80 columns by 24 rows is enough to play. More is better.

Apple's Terminal

It works, with three differences. One key stays held at a time. 24-bit colour arrived in macOS 26, and before that it shows 256. And the picture is a little blockier: its half blocks would leave a faint seam, so the games draw whole cells there instead.

Check yours

$ tty1 doctor

tty1 looks at the terminal it is running in and says what it found.
